## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH DEFINITION

A premium, human-designed travel memory book sold in US dollars to **foreign tourists leaving Uzbekistan**, distributed through **Uzbek tour operators** who already own the customer relationship, and printed locally in Tashkent. The customer sends photos from their Silk Road trip; a designer lays out the book within 72 hours; it is printed with lay-flat binding and delivered or shipped. The business is a **design service that outputs a physical object**, not a software product and not a print shop.

**What it explicitly is not:** a self-serve photobook editor, a children's storybook company, a multi-product gift brand, or a business targeting Uzbek domestic consumers priced in so'm. Each of these was considered and rejected — see §10.

## 3. THE PRODUCT

**Format:** Hardcover photo book, **A5 (14.8 × 21.0 cm)**, 20–60 pages.

> **Note on format.** A5 is exactly **half the page area of A4**, and every direct competitor is A4 or larger (Pixory: A4 hardcover 29.7 × 21.0 cm; PersonaBook: 29 × 22 cm). This is a deliberate divergence, not a default — see the decision entry in §10 and the pricing consequence in §6.

**Non-negotiable spec:**
- **Lay-flat binding.** Not perfect binding, not saddle stitch. This is the single most important production decision — see §9, Risk 3. Ask printers for it by name; a printer who doesn't recognise the term is disqualified.
- 200gsm+ silk-coated stock.
- Matte-laminated hard cover.
- Presentation box or protective sleeve (the unboxing is part of the product at this price point).

**Service wrapper (this is the actual product):**
1. Customer sends 40–80 photos via WhatsApp/Telegram/upload link.
2. Founder designs the layout — chronology, pacing, captions, cover.
3. Digital proof delivered within **72 hours**.
4. **Two rounds of revisions included.**
5. Print → QC → deliver.

**Deliberate design choice: the customer does not use an editor.** For the first ~50 books the founder does all design work manually. Rationale in §10.

**Local differentiation to develop:** Uzbek visual identity — suzani motifs, Registan/Bukhara/Khiva cover templates, Uzbek-tile-pattern endpapers, trilingual captions (EN/RU/UZ). No competitor can credibly offer this, and it converts a commodity photobook into a *souvenir of Uzbekistan specifically*.

## 4. CUSTOMER SEGMENTS

### Primary ICP — "The departing Silk Road tourist"
- European, North American, East Asian, or Gulf tourist on a 7–14 day Uzbekistan circuit (Tashkent → Samarkand → Bukhara → Khiva).
- Has spent $1,500–4,000+ on the trip. A $149 book is a rounding error on that.
- Has 600–2,000 phone photos and zero intention of ever organising them.
- **Will never return.** This trip is a one-time, high-significance life event.
- Peak emotional willingness-to-pay: the final 72 hours of the trip and the first two weeks after returning home.
- Pays in USD/EUR.

### Distribution partner (not the customer) — "The tour operator"
- Uzbek inbound tour agencies running group and private Silk Road circuits.
- Already owns the customer's trust, email, itinerary and often their photos.
- Motivation: differentiated upsell, higher perceived package value, memorable send-off gift, better reviews.
- **This is the channel, and it is the whole business in phase 1.**

### Secondary (phase 2, not now)
- Diaspora Uzbeks abroad buying family/heritage books.
- Expats and embassy/NGO staff in Tashkent (earn in USD, live locally).
- Uzbek weddings at the premium end — the only local segment where 1.5M so'm is normal spend.

### Explicitly out of scope for phase 1
Uzbek domestic mass consumers priced in so'm. Reason: see §6.

## 5. JOBS TO BE DONE

### Primary job
> **When** I finish the trip of a lifetime and come home with 1,400 photos I'll never look at again,
> **I want to** turn that trip into one physical object that lives in my house,
> **so I can** re-experience it, show it to people, and stop feeling like the whole thing is evaporating into my camera roll.

**Functional job:** Convert an unmanageable digital archive into a curated, finite, physical artifact.
**Emotional job:** Defend a significant memory against time and forgetting.
**Social job:** Have something to put on the coffee table that says who I am and where I've been. Show it to guests without asking them to look at a phone.

### Secondary jobs the product also serves
- **Gift job:** "Give a gift that proves I paid attention to what mattered to you."
- **Memorial job:** "Preserve someone or something I've lost." (Observed repeatedly in competitor reviews — customers making books for a deceased spouse and a deceased pet. This is a real and emotionally extreme use case.)
- **Operator's job:** "End my tour on a note the client remembers and tells friends about."

### Forces analysis

| Force | Content |
|---|---|
| **Push** (away from status quo) | Camera roll guilt; the trip fading; the photos are already buried under newer ones; "I always mean to do something with these." |
| **Pull** (toward the product) | A beautiful object; someone else does the hard part; it arrives finished. |
| **Anxiety** (blocking purchase) | "Will it look cheap?" "Will it arrive damaged?" "Is this website real?" "Will it get to my country?" "My photos aren't good enough." |
| **Habit** (holding them back) | "I'll just keep them on Instagram / Google Photos." "I'll do it myself someday." |

**Strategic implication:** The *anxiety* forces are the strongest in this category, and they are almost entirely addressed by (a) a physical sample the customer holds before buying, and (b) buying through a tour operator they already trust. **The distribution channel is not just cheap acquisition — it is anxiety removal.** This is the core insight of the business.

## 6. MARKET & PRICING REALITY

### Unit economics

| Line | Amount (UZS) | Amount (USD ≈12,700/$) |
|---|---|---|
| Retail price (local market rate) | 1,500,000 | ~$118 |
| **Target retail price (foreign tourist)** | — | **$149** |
| **Target wholesale price (to operator)** | — | **$99** |
| Founder-stated COGS | 500,000 | ~$39 |
| Realistic COGS incl. reprints/packaging/courier/scrapped proofs | 650,000–700,000 | ~$51–55 |
| **Gross profit per book** | ~800,000–1,000,000 | **~$60–79** |
| Gross margin | — | ~55–67% |

**Books/month to hit $1,000 profit:** 13–17.
**Books/month to hit $1,500 profit:** 20–25.

**A5 pricing consequence.** The 1.5M so'm local rate and the 500k COGS figure were gathered before the A5 decision and almost certainly reflect a larger format — **both need re-quoting at A5** (Open Question #4). Expect COGS to fall meaningfully; expect the *defensible* retail price to fall too, since the customer is holding an object half the area of a competitor's. Two possible resolutions, to be tested in the operator meetings:
- **Hold $149 and justify by everything except size** — thicker stock, lay-flat spine, presentation box, hand-designed layout, Uzbek-motif cover, made-in-country souvenir status. Higher margin, harder sell.
- **Drop to $89–119 and win on volume + impulse** — a price a tourist agrees to without deliberating, which suits a point-of-sale moment at the end of a tour. Lower margin per unit, but likely a far higher attach rate through operators, and the volume math still clears the side-income goal.

The second option deserves serious consideration: at A5, the product's natural role is a **high-attach-rate souvenir**, not a luxury coffee-table object. Do not decide this from a desk — put a physical A5 sample in front of three operators and ask what their clients would pay.

These are achievable numbers through 2–5 tour operators. This is the strongest fact about the project.

### Why local consumers are not the phase-1 market
Average monthly nominal wage, H1 2026: **7.09M so'm nationally; 12.01M so'm in Tashkent** (National Statistics Committee; figures exclude small enterprises and microfirms, so real medians are lower). A 1.5M so'm book is therefore **~12% of a monthly salary in Tashkent, ~21% nationally** — the local-purchasing-power equivalent of asking an American to pay ~$700 for a photobook. That is a luxury purchase, not a consumer product. Instagram ads to Uzbek consumers will generate engagement and near-zero conversion — a failure mode that *looks* like a marketing problem and tempts you to spend more on ads.

**Foreign tourists earning in hard currency solve this entirely. Price in USD.**

### Seasonality
Uzbek inbound tourism peaks **April–May and September–October** (summer is too hot). This is favourable and counter-cyclical to the global photobook category, which peaks at Christmas/Valentine's. **The September–October season is the immediate commercial window. Agencies must be signed in August.**

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

### Structural insight
**There is no technology moat in this category.** Pixory — the strongest operator — runs on **Printbox** (`getprintbox.com`), a licensed white-label photobook editor. PersonaBook's homepage is a near-verbatim Russian translation of Pixory's. Lumaria has testimonials copy-pasted from an apparel store ("I wear it at least twice a week" — on a photobook page).

The category is: **commodity product + rented software + brand + paid ads.** Everyone is buying customers from Meta at increasing cost. The only durable edges are (1) owned distribution, (2) production quality, (3) a market others can't physically serve.

### Category ceiling (calibration)
**Wonderbly** (formerly Lost My Name) is the best personalized-book business ever built: 4 million copies of one title, £46M buyout by Graphite Capital in 2021, acquired by Penguin Random House in June 2025. Its FY2020 accounts: **£26.1M sales, 66% gross margin, £2.9M operating profit — an ~11% operating margin** after 13 years and $22.5M of venture funding.

**Interpretation:** this is a real, durable, *modest-margin* category. It rewards operators, not visionaries. A profitable side income is a realistic goal; a fast-scaling startup is not.

### Known category failure modes (from competitor reviews — these are our opportunity)
1. **Pages falling out / pages glued together and tearing** — binding failure, the #1 complaint.
2. **Colour errors and blurry prints.**
3. **Editor bugs** — books arriving empty, checkout failures, accounts disappearing.
4. **Unreliable shipping times.**
5. **Support degrading into generic AI-written replies**; unstaffed live chat; complaints unresolved for 2+ months.
6. **Extreme emotional stakes.** Customers make these books for deceased spouses and pets. A defect here is not a defect — it is a wound, and it produces the review that destroys you.

## 8. POSITIONING & DIFFERENTIATION

**Positioning statement (working):**
> For travellers finishing a once-in-a-lifetime Silk Road journey, [NAME] turns their photos into a single hand-designed hardcover book — made in Uzbekistan, delivered before the memory fades. You send the photos. We do everything else.

**Do not use** any variant of "we sell beautiful moments of life as a book" / "preserve your memories" / "photographs are memories, photobooks tell stories." All three top competitors use this exact framing. It is category wallpaper and communicates nothing.

**The four real differentiators, in order of durability:**
1. **Owned distribution** — tour operators with existing client relationships. Zero CAC. Competitors pay Meta for every customer.
2. **Physical proximity** — printed and shipped from inside Uzbekistan; foreign competitors cannot serve this market well or fast.
3. **Human design, not self-serve software** — the customer buys an outcome, not a tool.
4. **Uzbek visual identity** — locally-rooted templates and materials nobody outside the country can authentically produce.

## 9. RISK REGISTER (ranked by what kills the project first)

### Risk 1 — Attention fragmentation. *Kills at ~week 10.*
Founder's stated instinct is to run travel books + children's books + love books across three websites and three funnels. With a solo founder and a ~10-week deadline, three parallel efforts means none reaches revenue and the project dies untested. **This is the most likely cause of death.** Diversification is what you do with profits, not with runway.
**Mitigation:** one product, one channel, one currency until the kill metric is passed. Everything else goes in a file dated 2027.

### Risk 2 — Selling to the wrong wallet. *Kills at ~month 3–4.*
Pointing this at Uzbek consumers in so'm produces traffic, saves, DMs asking price, and almost no orders — then tempts more ad spend in response.
**Mitigation:** USD pricing, foreign tourists, operator channel. Do not run consumer ads in phase 1.

### Risk 3 — Production defects and reprint costs. *Kills at ~month 4–6.*
The category's #1 complaint is binding failure. A memory book gets pressed flat on a table; perfect binding cracks. Every defect is a free reprint at our cost, and in this emotional category a defect also produces a devastating review.
**Mitigation:** lay-flat binding specified in writing; physically stress-test the first sample (bend it, press it flat, leave it in sun); budget 10–15% reprint rate into COGS from day one.

### Risk 4 — Disintermediation. *Kills at ~month 5–8, i.e. once it works.*
The founder sits between an agency that owns the customer and a printer that owns production. Either can cut him out with one phone call. Founder correctly self-assessed as having no moat here.
**Mitigation:** own the design layer and the template IP; consider an exclusivity clause with the printer; ask every operator directly what would make them keep you.

### Risk 5 — Cash conversion cycle. *Chronic; can kill even while profitable.*
Agencies will want 20–40% commission and 30–60 day terms while the printer demands payment up front.
**Mitigation:** prepaid or agency-collects-and-settles-weekly. Never carry an agency's receivables. Non-negotiable.

### Rejected (do not re-propose without new evidence)
| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Children's books + love books + travel books simultaneously | Fragmentation is Risk 1. Revisit in 2027 with profits, not runway. |
| Multiple websites and funnels | Same. |
| Customer self-serve editor / app producing a PDF | Turns the business into a print brokerage; imports every file-quality defect as our reprint cost; removes the differentiator. Build only after 50+ books sold manually. |

| Targeting Uzbek mass consumers in so'm (phase 1) | §6 purchasing power. |
| Competing on price against local polygraphy shops | Race to the bottom against businesses with lower overheads. Compete on design, channel and language. |
| "Original 3-month revenue-or-close" rule | Incoherent with a 6–10 week B2B cycle and a 6-month runway. |

## 11. OPEN QUESTIONS

1. **Brand name and domain.** Nothing chosen. Needs to work in English for foreign tourists (primary), be pronounceable, and ideally carry Uzbek/Silk Road resonance without being unspellable.
2. **Actual capital available in USD.** "6 months of survival" is a runway figure, not a working-capital figure. How much can be spent on samples, printing float, and the first inventory of packaging?
3. **Does PersonaBook actually ship to Uzbekistan?** Their FAQ says yes (CDEK, listing Uzbekistan explicitly); the founder says he checked and no competitor ships here. One of these is wrong. **Resolve by placing a real order to a Tashkent address (~$40, ~10 days).** This determines whether the market is genuinely uncontested.
4. **Which Tashkent printer, and can they do lay-flat at A5?** Three written quotes needed at 20/50/100 volumes, **quoted at A5 specifically** — the existing 1.5M price / 500k COGS figures predate the A5 decision and likely reflect a larger format. Small-format lay-flat binding is not universally offered; confirm before assuming. Also ask what each printer's *minimum* viable page count is at A5, since a thin small book feels flimsy.
5. **What does the tour operator actually want?** Commission %, white-label vs co-brand, who collects payment, whether they'd prefer it as a paid upsell or a bundled premium-package inclusion.
6. **What is Ertakcham actually doing?** Founder has local access; competitor with a real Uzbek Instagram funnel is worth understanding even in an adjacent category.
7. **Payment rails.** Foreign customers paying in USD from abroad — Payme/Click are domestic. Need Stripe/Paddle/Wise or agency-collects-cash. Legal entity implications (IP/OOO registration, currency controls, export of services).
8. **Delivery.** Hand to the tourist before departure (best — no shipping risk, instant gratification) vs. ship internationally after they return (better design time, but shipping cost/risk/customs). Likely both, priced differently.

## 12. PHASED ROADMAP

**Phase 0 — Validate (Aug 2026, 2 weeks).** One sample book. Three printer quotes. Five in-person operator meetings. PersonaBook test order. One landing page, EN + RU. Target: 2 signed agreements or 5 prepaid orders by 15 Sept.

**Phase 1 — Sell manually (Sept–Nov 2026, the autumn season).** Founder designs every book by hand. Target 13–25 books/month. Learn the printer's colour behaviour, the operators' objections, and what customers actually ask for. Collect reviews and photos obsessively — they are the phase-2 marketing asset.

**Phase 2 — Systematise (Dec 2026–Mar 2027, the off-season).** Template library. Uzbek-motif cover collection. Semi-automated intake. Hire or contract one designer. Decide whether a self-serve editor is worth licensing (Printbox and equivalents are rentable — do not build).

**Phase 3 — Expand (from Apr 2027).** Only after phase 1–2 economics are proven: adjacent products (wedding books, children's storybooks, diaspora/heritage books), Central Asian regional expansion (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan tourism circuits), or direct-to-tourist channels alongside operators.
